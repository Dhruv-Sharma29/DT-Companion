# ⭐ Key Insights

The running list. Newest at the top. Each line: the insight, then the note it lives in.

- **13 Sep 2026** — CB Insights March 2026: 70% ran out of capital, 43% poor product-market fit. Capital is the final cause; poor PMF (the wrong problem) is the root. The old "42% no market need" is superseded. → [[Team U Deck — Know Everything]]
- **13 Sep 2026** — "2 lakh startups isn't your market" — correct: startups are where users are heading, students with intent (32.5%) are the users, incubators (1,100+) are the buyers. → [[Team U Deck — Know Everything]]
- **13 Sep 2026** — The support gap: >50% of startups now come from Tier-II/III cities, but 48% of incubators sit in Tier-I, and India has ~0.8 incubators per million vs 8–10 in the US/UK/China. Student intent is outrunning support. → [[Core Statement]] · [[Sources]]
- **13 Sep 2026** — Frame info management and process as *causes*, and end the statement on the *consequence* (untested assumptions, generic AI answers, wrong problem). That keeps it from reading as a Notion problem. → [[Core Statement]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Where mentors are scarce, the product asks the questions a mentor would; where they exist, it shows them the reasoning. The tier 2–3 mentorship gap is a reason to exist, not just a market stat. → [[Core Statement]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Define the user by intent, not degree: an engineering *project* isn't business-focused, but an engineering student who wants to start a company is. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — In a course the wrong problem costs a grade; in a startup it's fatal. The core problem becomes the venture's central risk, not a teaching concern. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — "Change the problem, the idea, or the execution?" is literally pivot-or-persevere. The product answers the question every founder dreads, with a record instead of a gut feeling. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — "Reasoning becomes gradeable" survives the pivot as "reasoning becomes fundable". Mentors and incubators see the pitch, never the reasoning. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — LLMs return the most probable answer, and the most probable answer is the least unique. They're convergence machines — the failure we're fighting, automated. → [[Why Not ChatGPT — The Average Answer]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Uniqueness = your first-hand anomaly × the landscape's gap. The generic answer ignores the first and doesn't map the second. → [[Why Not ChatGPT — The Average Answer]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Segment pivot to MBA turns the weakest point (unfelt problem) into a strong one: they compete on differentiation, so "same idea as everyone" is a named pain. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Engineer → MBA is a shift from problem-solving to problem-setting. Engineers jump to building — premature convergence in its most well-trained form. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — A uniqueness feature that fabricates competitors would be self-refuting. Landscape intel must be search-grounded, linked, and marked secondhand. → [[Landscape Intel]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Sell the pain they feel, deliver the benefit they don't as a side effect. Students don't feel weak reasoning; they do feel dread of the crit. → [[Making It Attractive]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Mock Crit flips the product's emotional charge: a weak link stops being criticism and becomes a service — better you found it than the panel. → [[Making It Attractive]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Anything you reward, students will produce. Points reward volume — exactly what AI made cheap. Only reward what can't be faked cheaply. → [[Making It Attractive]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Reward graduation, not activity: "you asked three of the questions the app used to ask." It can't be faked by using the app more. → [[Making It Attractive]]
- **11 Sep 2026** — Concentrated friction feels intentional; diffuse friction feels like a bad app. Effortless everywhere except commitment. → [[Making It Attractive]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — Native wins at the *edges* — physical world, OS integration, data's legal status — and competes fine in the middle. Our middle is text on a screen, so the whole platform argument is an argument about edges. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — The share sheet is a structural answer to capture overhead, the failure that killed IBIS. Practically the most decisive native advantage. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — We import groupings, we do not provide grouping. No clustering canvas — that's Miro's ground and not the gap. → [[Process Coverage — How Far the App Helps]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — A photograph is the one entry type whose provenance is self-evident and that AI cannot fabricate. That's the camera's real job — not scanning affinity walls. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — Name the move *after* they've made it, not before. A label lands when there's an experience under it; taught up front it's a term with nothing attached. → [[Teaching Model]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — The honest response to weak input is to *show* it's weak. A ledger reading 90% secondhand isn't broken — it's the most valuable thing you could tell a team about to build on it. → [[Input Requirements]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — A team using this should change their mind *more* often, earlier and more cheaply. "Fewer assumption changes" is the opposite of the goal. → [[Business — DFV, BMC, Segments, Impact]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — Desirability is real but sits with the *secondary* user. Say that plainly; three-for-three on DFV isn't believed. → [[Business — DFV, BMC, Segments, Impact]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — On-device inference means near-zero marginal cost per student — a structural advantage in the BMC, not just a privacy story. → [[Business — DFV, BMC, Segments, Impact]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — Honest ceiling: it makes weak reasoning *visible*. It can't create domain expertise, fix assessment, or make a team care. → [[Business — DFV, BMC, Segments, Impact]]
- **10 Sep 2026** — If the ledger assembles the final pitch, it pays off a third time — and students maintain it because it writes their submission. Best adoption argument yet. → [[Backlog]] #13
- **10 Sep 2026** — "Fewer assumption changes" is the wrong target: changing an assumption against evidence is the process working. Aim at fewer *unnoticed* assumptions and fewer *late* reversals. → [[Backlog]] #8
- **9 Sep 2026** — We're not replacing or reinventing DT — we're adding the missing method to the one stage that never had one. The process we work *inside* is the full loop; the step we *support* is one convergence. → [[Process Coverage — How Far the App Helps]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — On-device AI isn't a privacy *feature*, it's a *permission* difference: a tool that can't hold the real data can't teach judgement about the real data. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Answer "why not web" by inverting it: what could the product no longer *promise*? Feature lists lose that argument; four load-bearing losses win it. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Volunteering what native *doesn't* differentiate (performance, UI, camera, push) is what makes the four real claims credible. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Browser storage on iOS can be silently evicted; a semester-long record that can vanish is not a record. → [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Coverage: deep before the prototype, thin at it, a second peak after it. Coaching at Define, record across the whole loop. → [[Process Coverage — How Far the App Helps]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — The ledger's value at Define is immediate but modest; its value at Iterate is what makes keeping it *rational*. Without that payoff the ledger is homework. → [[Process Coverage — How Far the App Helps]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — "Change the problem, the idea, or the execution?" is answerable mechanically: find where the disconfirmed assumption lives. Nobody can trace it without a record. → [[Process Coverage — How Far the App Helps]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Helping at Ideate would contradict the thesis — generating ideas is exactly what AI made free. The only legitimate job there is checking the idea answers the committed problem. → [[Process Coverage — How Far the App Helps]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — A reasoning chain is a *structure*; read one card at a time it isn't seen at all. "Make reasoning visible" is a claim about area. → [[Platform Decision — iPad App]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Silent divergence *then collision* is a shared-viewing event. Four people gather around an iPad; nobody gathers around a phone, and a laptop puts one person behind the screen. → [[Platform Decision — iPad App]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Comparison requires simultaneity. Sequence is what lets an unsupported claim pass unnoticed. → [[Platform Decision — iPad App]]
- **9 Sep 2026** — Handwriting is slower than typing, and slowness is on our side — the Pencil supplies deliberate friction without the tool having to nag. → [[Platform Decision — iPad App]]
