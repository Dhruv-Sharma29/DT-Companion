# 📐 Platform Decision — iPad App

**9 Sep 2026.** Decision: **native iPad app, iPad only.**
Supersedes the earlier iPhone-first and web-first positions.

## The repositioning this implies #key
Do this consciously, because it changes which argument defends the product.

| | iPhone framing (dropped) | **iPad framing (chosen)** |
|---|---|---|
| The product is | capture + nudge in the moment | **the surface where reasoning is made visible** |
| Core claim | provenance is captured, not reconstructed | **a team can see and defend its own thinking** |
| Payoff moment | a nudge arrives as you leave an interview | **the team looks at its chain and sees where it jumped** |

Provenance still matters — it is *what* the ledger records. It is simply no longer the
argument for the device. The device argument is now **legibility and shared attention**.

## Why iPad is the right surface

### 1. Reasoning must be seen whole, and a chain needs area #key
The promise is *make reasoning visible*. Visible how? A claim, the evidence under it, the
alternatives rejected, the assumption still untested — that is a **structure**, and a structure
read one card at a time is not seen at all. The payoff moment of this product is a team looking
at its own argument laid out. **That moment needs area.** A phone can capture; it cannot
reveal.

### 2. Design Thinking is done by teams, and teams need a shared surface #key
[[AI Nudging — Interaction Design]] specifies *silent divergence, then collision* — each member
interprets alone, then the tool shows where they disagree. **The collision is inherently a
shared-viewing event.** Four people can gather around an iPad on a table. Nobody gathers around
a phone. A phone is a private device; this is a group act. #insight

### 3. The core interaction is literally two panes
Toulmin's structure — claim *beside* the data that warrants it — is a side-by-side. So is
"note 12 contradicts note 31." So is "here is your conclusion, here is what it rests on."
The central interaction is comparison, and **comparison requires simultaneity.** The phone
forces sequence, and sequence is what lets an unsupported claim pass unnoticed.

### 4. Apple Pencil — and the friction is a feature #insight
Earlier this note argued Pencil serves only clustering, which is not the gap. That was too
quick. Handwriting and annotation do real reasoning work: circling the weak link, marking a
transcript, sketching a frame. And critically — **writing by hand is slower than typing, and
slowness is on our side.** [[The Automation Paradox]] wants deliberate friction; the Pencil
supplies it honestly, without the tool having to nag.

### 5. One device, one layout, one demo
For a course project this matters more than it sounds. A universal app is two layouts, two
sets of edge cases, two things to polish. **One target is far more likely to ship well** and to
demo without apology.

### 6. Institutional fit
Design and engineering programmes issue and expect iPads. An instructor mandating a tool
([[Scope, Platform & Users]]) is likelier to be mandating it on a device the department already
supports.

## What this costs — name it before you're asked #key

### 1. Capture in the field gets weaker
An iPad held between you and an interviewee is a barrier — a real, documented concern in
ethnographic practice. Photos and audio are more awkward. **Mitigation:** the app is honest
about being the *sensemaking* surface, and accepts material captured however the team already
captures it. Provenance is then **asked for at import** — *when was this, where, who said it* —
rather than harvested from sensors. Weaker, but explicit, and arguably better teaching: the
team has to *state* provenance instead of having it silently attached.

### 2. The nudge-at-the-moment mechanic largely goes
An iPad is not in your pocket, so a notification will not reach someone walking out of an
interview. **Mitigation:** nudges move to *session start* and *at commitment inside the app* —
still the right moments per [[AI Nudging — Interaction Design]], just fewer of them.
Do not claim the in-the-moment nudge in a pitch; you cannot deliver it.

### 3. Ubiquity #question
Not every student owns an iPad. This matters for a stage-3 course pilot and for who can try a
demo. Check it before committing — see [[Open Questions]].

### 4. The anti-web argument gets harder, not easier #key
> [!warning] The single most important consequence.
> Every reason given above — big screen, side-by-side, shared viewing, review surface — is a
> reason a **website on a laptop** also satisfies. The iPhone case rested on things the web
> genuinely cannot do (sensors, pocket presence, push at a moment). The iPad case does not.

So the *"why not a website?"* answer has to be rebuilt on the four things that survive:

1. **Apple Pencil** — direct annotation on evidence; no web equivalent that feels right.
2. **Touch-direct manipulation** of a reasoning structure, not mouse-and-scroll.
3. **On-device AI and privacy** — interview material never leaves the device.
4. **Offline in the field** — full function with no connection.

Full treatment, including what *not* to claim: [[Native vs Web — The Differentiators]].

## Roadmap
1. **iPad** — the ledger, the review surface, the nudge loop, Pencil annotation. This is the product.
2. **iPhone** — later, a thin *capture-only* companion feeding the same ledger. Recovers the
   provenance-at-capture story without splitting the main build.
3. **Instructor view** — the genuinely desktop-shaped job, for a different user.
