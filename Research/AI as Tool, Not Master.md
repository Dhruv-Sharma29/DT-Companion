# 🔧 AI as Tool, Not Master

**8 Sep 2026** — the principle underneath [[The Automation Paradox]].
The old phrasing is *"a good servant but a bad master"* (said of fire, and of money).

## The one-line version #key
> A tool **extends** your judgement. A master **replaces** it. AI cannot be a master
> because it **bears no consequence for being wrong** — and in design, consequence is the
> only thing that makes a decision real.

## Why it is a good tool
- **Widens the search space** — more framings, more analogies, more options than a novice generates alone.
- **Removes drudgery** — transcription, tidying, first-pass clustering, reformatting.
- **Tireless devil's advocate** — will argue the opposite side without ego or fatigue. This is
  its most underused mode, and the one closest to what sensemaking actually needs. #insight
- **Lends knowledge you don't have** — partial substitute for the missing domain baseline
  (factor #2 in [[Why Students Struggle — Causal Factors]]).

## Why it is a bad master
| Property                                  | Consequence                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Optimises for **plausibility**, not truth | Coherent ≠ correct, and coherence is what you feel            |
| **Bears no cost** for being wrong         | Accountability cannot be delegated to it                      |
| **Was not in the room**                   | It can recombine, never observe — see provenance collapse     |
| **Hides uncertainty**                     | Confident register regardless of actual confidence            |
| **Converges by nature**                   | Hands you *an* answer; design needs divergence held open      |
| **Removes the friction**                  | The struggle was the learning; skip it and no judgement forms |

> [!important] The failure isn't AI being wrong. It's AI being *plausible* while the human
> stops checking — because there is no longer any felt reason to.

## The design rule this yields
Let AI do the **noticing**. The human must do the **committing**.
Anything that ends in a commitment — this is the problem, this insight matters, we build
this next — stays with the person, and the tool's job is to make that commitment *visible
and costly enough to be real*.
