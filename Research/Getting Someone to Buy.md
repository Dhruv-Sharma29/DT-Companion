# 💰 Getting Someone to Buy

**14 Sep 2026.** How to get a real buyer for a product that doesn't exist yet.

## Who actually pays #key
- **Not students.** They'll use it, but most won't pay.
- **Incubators, E-Cells, entrepreneurship centres.** They have budgets and cohorts, and it's their job to produce better founders. → [[User — Student Startup Aspirants]]
- The person who says yes is usually the **incubator manager / CEO** or the **faculty coordinator
  of the E-Cell or IIC**, not the institution in general.

## Sell before you build #key
You don't need an app to get a commitment. Go in this order:

| Step | What you do | What you ask for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Listen | Talk to 5 incubator managers / E-Cell coordinators about their cohorts. Don't pitch. | 20 minutes |
| 2 · Show | Come back with the paper version: run a Mock Crit on 2–3 of *their* founders' ideas | One session with their founders |
| 3 · Pilot | Offer to run it free with one cohort for a few weeks | A cohort, and an agreed measure of success |
| 4 · Commit | If the pilot works, ask them to pay for the next cohort | A signed letter of intent, or payment |

**A buyer is someone who gives you something costly** — money, a cohort, their founders' time, a
signature, an introduction to another incubator. "Sounds great, keep us posted" is not a buyer.

## What to ask a buyer (step 1)
1. How do you decide which founders to take into your programme?
2. What do most founders get wrong in their first few months? Tell me about a recent one.
3. How much mentor time does each founder get? What happens between sessions?
4. How do you know whether a founder has really understood their users?
5. What does your incubator get measured on, and by whom?
6. What have you already tried to improve founder quality? What did it cost?

Question 5 matters most: whatever they're measured on is what they'll pay to improve.

## Pitch in the buyer's terms, not the student's
| Student cares about | Buyer cares about |
|---|---|
| Is my problem real, taken, mine? | **More founders who get funded or win competitions** |
| Not failing my crit | **Less mentor time wasted** on unvalidated ideas |
| A pitch that holds up | **Visible outcomes** to report to whoever funds or rates them |

One-line pitch to an incubator:
> *"Your founders arrive with evidence behind their problem, so your mentors spend their time on
> the hard questions instead of the obvious ones."*

## Pricing — test, don't guess
Don't pick a number yet. Try options during step 3:
- **Per cohort** (simplest for incubators to budget)
- **Per founder team per semester**
- **Free for students, paid mentor/incubator view**

Ask: *"If this worked, what would it replace in your budget?"* The answer tells you what it's worth.

## What to check (hypotheses, not facts)
- [ ] Do incubator managers control a budget they could spend on this? #question
- [ ] Is incubator/IIC performance measured in a way this could help report? Ask — don't assume. #question
- [ ] Will students pay anything at all — e.g. Mock Crit before a competition? #question

## For the presentation — "Who pays?"
> *"Incubators and E-Cells. They want founders who come in with a validated problem, because it
> saves mentor time and produces more fundable startups. Our plan is to run a free pilot with one
> incubator cohort first and ask for payment only if the results show up. We haven't tested
> willingness to pay yet."*
