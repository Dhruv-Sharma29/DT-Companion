# 🎙️ User Interview Guide — Do They Actually Have This Problem?

**14 Sep 2026.** A question set for student startup aspirants that checks whether they have
the problem **without telling them what the problem is**. Also closes the biggest gap in
[[Team U Deck — Know Everything]]: *no primary user research yet*.

---

## The rules — read before every interview #key

1. **Never mention the idea, the app, or the problem.** Once they know what you're hoping to
   hear, they'll say it to be nice.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time…" gives facts.
   "Would you use…" gives kind lies.
3. **Ask for specifics.** A real story has a date, a name, a tool, a decision. "Usually" and
   "generally" mean you haven't got the story yet. Ask: *"Can you think of one specific time?"*
4. **Ask them to show you.** "Can you show me the notes / the doc / the chat?" What they have
   beats what they say.
5. **Let silence work.** After they answer, wait three seconds. The useful part often comes next.
6. **Don't correct or teach.** If they're doing it "wrong", that's the data.
7. **Words to avoid:** *AI dependence, assumption, validate, struggle, problem, would you,
   do you think, isn't it hard to.*

**Three follow-ups that work on any answer**
- *"What happened next?"*
- *"Why did you do it that way?"*
- *"How did you decide?"*

---

## 0 · Screener — is this person our user? (2 min)

- What are you studying, and which year?
- Are you working on anything outside your coursework right now — a project, an idea, a startup?
- *(If yes)* How far along is it? Who's working on it with you?
- Have you been part of an E-Cell, incubator, hackathon or startup competition?

✅ **Include:** has an idea or venture they're actively pursuing, or has pursued one in the past year.
❌ **Exclude:** only doing required coursework with no intent to build.

---

## 1 · Warm-up — the story of the idea (5 min)

- Tell me about the idea you're working on. How did it start?
- What were you doing when you first thought of it?
- What's happened since then — roughly, what have you done so far?

> *Listen for:* where the idea came from. Something they saw happen to real people, or something
> that sounded like a good idea? Did they start building early?

---

## 2 · How they chose the problem (8 min) · *jump to solution / pick what sounds good*

- Before you settled on this idea, what other ideas or directions were you considering?
- What made you pick this one over the others?
- When did you first feel sure this was worth working on? What happened at that moment?
- Who did you talk to before you started building? Tell me about one of those conversations.
- If you started again today, what would you do differently at the beginning?

> ✅ **Problem confirmed:** chose it because it "felt right" or "sounded strong"; started building
> before talking to anyone; can't name the alternatives they considered.
> ❌ **Problem not present:** can name specific people they spoke to and what they learned, and can
> explain why they dropped the alternatives.

---

## 3 · Research and where it lives (8 min) · *scattered research*

- Walk me through the research you did for this idea. Where did you start?
- Where is all of that now — the notes, recordings, links, findings? **Can you show me?**
- If I asked you for the most important thing a user told you, how would you find it?
- When you came back to your research after a break, what was that like?
- What tools did you use along the way? How did information move between them?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** research spread across WhatsApp, Notes, Docs, ChatGPT, photos, screenshots;
> takes a while (or fails) to find a specific quote; things lost or forgotten.
> ❌ **Not present:** one organised place, and they find the quote quickly.

---

## 4 · Telling evidence from conclusions (8 min) · *observed vs assumed — the core*

- Tell me about the people you spoke to. What's one thing someone actually said that stuck with you?
- *(Pick a statement from their notes or pitch)* Where did that come from?
- What's something you believe about your users that you haven't checked yet?
- Has anything you believed about your users turned out to be wrong? What happened?

> **Best signal in the whole interview:** they present a belief as if a user said it, then when you
> ask *"where did that come from?"* they pause, check, or admit it was their own guess or an AI
> summary.
>
> ✅ **Confirmed:** can't separate what users said from what the team concluded; can't name an
> untested belief (because they don't see one).
> ❌ **Not present:** names clear sources, and readily lists what's still untested.

### 🧪 Optional exercise (10 min) — the strongest test
Ask them to open their own research notes or summary, then:
> *"Could you go through these and mark each line: did someone actually say or do this, or is it
> something you — or a tool — concluded?"*

Watch, and don't help. **Hesitation, arguments within the team, or lots of "not sure" is direct
evidence of the problem.** Count the lines they can't classify.

---

## 5 · How AI fits in (6 min) · *reliance on AI, generic answers*

*Keep this neutral. Many students feel judged here, so don't imply AI use is bad.*

- Which tools helped you most while working on the idea? What did you use each one for?
- Tell me about the last time you asked an AI tool something about your idea. What did you ask?
  **Can you show me the chat?**
- What did you do with what it gave you?
- Did you ever compare notes with another team working in a similar space? What did you notice?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** used AI to generate or pick the problem, or to summarise interviews; used the
> output fairly directly; noticed other teams had similar ideas.
> ❌ **Not present:** AI used only for mechanical tasks (grammar, formatting, code), and the
> problem came from their own observation.

---

## 6 · "Has someone already built this?" (5 min) · *crowded problem, discovered late*

- How did you find out what else exists in this space?
- When did you do that — before or after you started building?
- Tell me about the moment you found something similar. What did you do?
- How would you explain what makes yours different? *(Listen to how easily this comes.)*

> ✅ **Confirmed:** found a competitor late, often by accident; can't clearly explain the
> difference, or the difference is "ours is better / simpler".
> ❌ **Not present:** did competitor research early and explains a specific difference.

---

## 7 · Process — knowing what to do next (5 min) · *no structured process*

- At the start, how did you decide what to do first?
- Was there a point where you didn't know what to do next? What did you do?
- Did you follow any method or framework? How closely? Where did you drop it?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** stages done out of order, steps skipped, "we just started building";
> the method was learned but not used.
> ❌ **Not present:** describes a sequence they actually followed.

---

## 8 · Teamwork (5 min) · *collaboration, shared understanding*

- Tell me about a time your team disagreed about the idea or the users. How did it get resolved?
- How does everyone on the team know what the others found?
- When a new teammate joined, or someone missed a week, how did they catch up?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** disagreements settled by the loudest or most senior person, or by voting;
> no shared record; re-explaining things repeatedly.
> ❌ **Not present:** disagreements settled by going back to what users said.

---

## 9 · Mentors and feedback (5 min) · *mentor gap*

- Who gives you feedback on the idea? How often?
- Tell me about the most useful feedback you've had. What did they ask?
- Tell me about the toughest question you got — in a pitch, crit or review. How did you answer?
- If you're unsure about something, who do you ask? What if they're not available?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** little or no regular mentor access; tough questions exposed gaps they hadn't
> noticed; they turn to ChatGPT or friends when no mentor is around.
> ❌ **Not present:** regular, easily available mentor feedback.

---

## 10 · When something failed (5 min) · *pivot or persevere*

- Tell me about something you tried that didn't work — a feature, a test, a pitch.
- How did you figure out why it didn't work?
- What did you change afterwards? How did you decide what to change?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** unsure whether the problem, the idea or the execution was wrong; changed things
> by guessing or started over.
> ❌ **Not present:** traced it to a specific belief that turned out wrong.

---

## 11 · How much it matters (4 min) · *is the pain real enough?*

- Of everything you've described, what took the most time or caused the most frustration?
- What have you tried to fix that? *(Tools bought, templates, courses, asking seniors.)*
- Have you spent money or a lot of time on anything to help with this?

> **Pain is real if they've already tried to solve it.** Workarounds, paid tools, courses, templates
> — anything costly. If they've never tried anything, the problem may exist but not matter enough.

---

## 12 · Close (2 min)

- Is there anything about working on your idea that I should have asked but didn't?
- Who else should I talk to — someone else working on an idea?
- Would it be okay if I got back in touch later?

> **Commitment signals:** introduces you to others, shares their actual notes, agrees to a follow-up.
> Those count for more than "that sounds useful".

---

## 10-question version #key

1. Tell me about the idea you're working on. How did it start?
2. What other ideas were you considering, and what made you pick this one?
3. Who did you talk to before you started building? Tell me about one of those conversations.
4. Where is all your research now? *Can you show me?*
5. What's one thing a user actually said that stuck with you? → *Where did that come from?*
6. What's something you believe about your users that you haven't checked yet?
7. The last time you used an AI tool on your idea, what did you ask, and what did you do with the answer?
8. When did you find out about similar products, and what did you do then?
9. Tell me about something that didn't work. How did you decide what to change?
10. Of everything you've described, what's been most frustrating, and what have you tried to fix it?

## Short version — 15 minutes

If time is tight, ask only these:
1. How did you pick this idea over the others?
2. Where does your research live? *Can you show me?*
3. What's one thing a user actually said? — *then* — Where did that come from?
4. What's something you believe about your users that you haven't checked?
5. Last time you used AI on your idea — what did you ask, and what did you do with the answer?
6. When did you find out about similar products? What did you do?
7. Tell me about something that didn't work. How did you decide what to change?
8. What have you already tried to make any of this easier?

## Mentor / incubator version (secondary user)

- Tell me about the last founder pitch you reviewed. What did you ask them?
- How do you tell whether a founder has really understood their users?
- What's the most common mistake you see in early teams? Tell me about a recent example.
- When a founder says "users want X", how do you check it?
- How much time do you get with each founder? What happens between sessions?
- What do founders bring you — what do you actually get to see?

> ✅ **Confirmed:** mentors see pitch decks, not the reasoning behind them; limited time per founder;
> the same mistakes come up across cohorts.

**After 5–8 interviews:** a hypothesis confirmed in most interviews is strong evidence. One
confirmed in only one or two should be dropped or reworded, and the product and deck should
change to match. *That's the point of doing this.*

> [!important] Record exact quotes
> Write down what people actually said, word for word, and mark your own interpretations
> separately. Otherwise your interview notes end up with the same problem you're studying.
