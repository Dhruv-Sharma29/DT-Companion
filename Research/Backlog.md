### 7. Start point — what material is required, and is it valid?
What a team must arrive with. **Also an interesting design problem:** if the input is already
AI-laundered text with no provenance, does the product still work? Needs an explicit answer. #question

### 8. Reducing assumption churn
*"Make the journey easier so changes of assumptions are fewer."*
Careful — fewer changes is **not** obviously the goal. Changing an assumption when evidence
contradicts it is the process working. The real target is **fewer *unnoticed* assumptions and
fewer late reversals**, not fewer revisions. Worth thinking through properly. #question

### 9. Impact
What changes if this works — for a team, for a cohort, for how DT is taught.

### 10. AI nudge: teaching or content? #question
New distinction, not yet resolved. Does the app **teach the method** (explains the Ladder of
Inference, builds transferable skill) or only **prompt in context** (asks the right question,
teaches nothing explicitly)? Affects fading, the instructor's value, and the entire claim to
be an educational tool. **Decide before the feature list.**

## 🟣 New idea — worth keeping

### 13. The ledger generates the final pitch #key
*"The information uploaded creates a final pitch, evolving over time."*

Strong idea, and strategically more than a feature: it gives the ledger a **third** payoff.

| When | What the ledger returns |
|---|---|
| At **Define** | better thinking, once |
| At **Iterate** | what to change when a test fails |
| At the **end** | the pitch, assembled from decisions already recorded |

That third one may be the best *adoption* argument in the whole project: students maintain the
record because it **writes their final submission**. It converts the ledger from homework into
the thing that saves them a week. #insight

Design caution: it must **assemble what was recorded**, not generate persuasive text. The
moment it writes the argument for them, it becomes the thing we are arguing against
([[The Automation Paradox]]).
