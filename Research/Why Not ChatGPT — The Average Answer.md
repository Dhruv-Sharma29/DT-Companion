# 🎯 Why Not ChatGPT — The Average Answer

**11 Sep 2026.** A stronger answer to *"why won't they just use ChatGPT?"* than the one we
had. Replaces the "withholds the answer" argument as the lead.

## The claim #key
> An LLM returns the **most probable** answer — the consensus of everything it was trained on.
> The most probable answer is, by definition, **the least unique one.**

It will give you a competent, reasonable, *general* reading of your research. So will it give
the team next to you. Twenty teams researching similar areas get twenty variations of the same
problem statement.

**LLMs are convergence machines.** And premature convergence is the exact failure this project
is about ([[Problem Definition]]). ChatGPT doesn't just fail to help — it pulls every team
toward the *same* first plausible interpretation. #insight

## Where uniqueness actually lives #key
Not in the model. It lives in two places the model cannot reach:

1. **Your own first-hand anomaly** — the observation that *doesn't fit* the generic story. The
   workaround nobody expected, the user who did the opposite, the thing that surprised you.
   The model has never seen it; it was not in the training data; it happened in your room.
2. **The gap in the landscape** — where existing solutions stop. You cannot know an angle is
   unique without knowing what already exists. → [[Landscape Intel]]

> **Uniqueness = your anomaly × the landscape's gap.**
> The generic answer ignores the first and doesn't map the second.

## What the companion does about it
Consistent with *AI notices, the human commits* ([[AI as Tool, Not Master]]):

- **Surfaces anomalies** in the team's own evidence — *"these three observations don't fit the
  story you've settled on."* Noticing is legitimate AI work.
- **Flags the generic** — when a draft problem statement reads like the default reading of the
  domain, says so: *"this is the obvious framing — what did you see that the obvious framing
  misses?"*
- **Maps the landscape** so the team can see where the open areas are.

What it **never** does: generate the unique angle for them. A model generating "unique
insights" produces the average of unique-sounding insights — the same trap one level up.

## Honest note on "old output"
The *general* point is strong. The *old* point — training cutoffs — is weaker: models with
search can be current. Lead with **average**, not **old**; an expert will knock down the
second and may take the first with it.

## One line
> *ChatGPT gives every team the same good answer. The valuable answer is the one only your
> research could produce — and it's hiding in the observations that don't fit.*
