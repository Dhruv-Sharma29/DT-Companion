# 🏷️ Naming

**9 Sep 2026.** Two separate questions — the category it belongs to, and what the product
is called.

## The category
Already settled in [[Terminology & Prior Art]]:

- **What the artifact is:** a **reasoning ledger** — a dated, append-only record of what the
  team observed, concluded, and what each conclusion rests on.
- **What the field calls this kind of thing:** **design rationale** (the academic category),
  or **metacognitive scaffolding for design practice** (the pedagogical framing).

Use *reasoning ledger* when explaining it, *design rationale* when placing it in the literature.

## Criteria for the product name
1. A real word — short, sayable, no invented spelling.
2. Names what it **produces** or **protects**, not what it does to you. Nothing that sounds
   like nagging.
3. Has a good answer to *"why that name?"* — ideally rooted in the theory. Free credibility.
4. Survives the follow-up: *"and what does it do?"* in one sentence.

## Candidates

| Name | Where it comes from | Why it works | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warrant** | Toulmin — the element that *justifies* a claim from its evidence | Exactly what the product produces. Short, serious, and the origin story lands with anyone who knows argumentation | Slight legal / police connotation |
| **Grounds** | Toulmin again — the data a claim stands on; also *well-grounded* | Plain, warm, no jargon. *"What are your grounds?"* is a natural question a team can ask each other | Common word, harder to make distinctive |
| **Rung** | Argyris — the Ladder of Inference | Tied directly to the core mechanic: naming the rung you skipped. Short and unusual | Needs the ladder explained first |
| **Dwell** | The affective insight — premature closure is discomfort-avoidance | Captures *staying with* ambiguity, which is the real ask | Reads poetic rather than evidential |
| **Fieldbook** | The lab-notebook lineage | Familiar, honest, obviously a record | Generic; several products near it |

## Recommendation #key
**Warrant.**

- It names the thing the product exists to create: the link between what you saw and what you
  claim.
- The etymology answer is strong in an academic setting — *Toulmin's term for what entitles you
  to move from evidence to conclusion* — and this is a project that will be judged on rigour.
- It pairs cleanly with the one-line description:
  > **Warrant** — it keeps a record of what your conclusions rest on, and asks the question you
  > didn't.

**Grounds** is the safer, friendlier alternative if *Warrant* reads too formal for students.
